Building a five-minute daily math habit

Math practice tips · 4 min read · November 10, 2026

Arithmetic is like exercise: five minutes every day beats two hours once a week. Here is how to turn short practice into a lasting habit.

Why short and often?

Recalling something just as you're about to forget it makes the memory stronger. This is spaced repetition. A five-minute game each day naturally brings back yesterday's facts. And short sessions end before boredom sets in, so children want to come back tomorrow.

Four ways to build the habit

1. Fix the time

"When there's time" usually means never. Attach practice to something that already happens every day, like right after dinner.

2. A clear finish line

A 1 min Challenge lasts a minute and a 60-Question Race takes two to four. Goals like "three 1 min Challenges today" have a clear end.

3. Watch the records grow

SumSum Playground saves your best record for each game. Seeing it beat last week's is a big motivator. Stickers on a calendar help too.

4. Praise effort, not scores

Instead of "100 points, amazing!", try "fewer mistakes than yesterday" or "you didn't skip a day". Scores go up and down, but consistency grows when it is noticed.

A sample week

Mon · Wed · FriThree 1 min Challenges (speed)
Tue · ThuOne Falling Problems game and look at the review list together (accuracy)
WeekendOne Ranked game to check the week's progress

Stop while it's fun — If a fun game turns into an hour, it can feel like a chore the next day. Stopping while you still want more is the secret.
