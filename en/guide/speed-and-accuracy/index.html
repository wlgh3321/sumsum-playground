Speed or accuracy? Cutting down careless mistakes

Math practice tips · 4 min read · November 1, 2026

"I knew it, but I got it wrong." Mistakes aren't bad luck; they are habits, and once you know the cause you can reduce them.

Three kinds of mistakes

  • Not knowing: a shaky times table or not yet understanding carrying. Go back to the concept.
  • Rushing: knowing how, but misreading a number or forgetting to carry.
  • Copying: the answer in your head was right but you typed the digits in the wrong order (54 as 45).

The review list on the SumSum Playground results screen shows the right answer next to yours. Sort each mistake into a type together with your child.

Accuracy first, speed later

Speeding up first locks in wrong habits. Try this order:

  1. Slow and correct: reach 90% accuracy at a slow speed.
  2. Steady: check that the accuracy holds for a few days.
  3. A little faster: raise the speed or range by one step. If accuracy drops, step back.

Small habits that prevent mistakes

Estimate for one second

Thinking "about 50" first catches answers that are way off, like 5 or 500.

Check the last digit

Recalculate just the ones digits and compare with your answer's last digit. See the True or False guide for more.

Look before you press OK

Glancing at your typed answer before OK removes most copying mistakes.

Choosing a game — For children who rush, Beat the Clock is great practice: mistakes cost a lot, so accuracy is what keeps you going.
