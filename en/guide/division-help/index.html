Helping a child who finds division hard

Math practice tips · 4 min read · November 10, 2026

Division is the last operation children learn and often the hardest. Usually the real problem is a weak link to multiplication.

Two meanings of division

  • Sharing: 12 sweets shared equally by 3 children: how many each? → 12 ÷ 3 = 4
  • Grouping: 12 sweets put into bags of 3: how many bags? → 12 ÷ 3 = 4

Acting out both with real objects shows that division isn't just a symbol to memorise.

Connect it to multiplication

The surest way to get faster is to turn division into a multiplication question.

35 ÷ 5 = ? → 5 × ? = 35 → 5 × 7 = 35 → the answer is 7

Say this switch out loud a few times and it soon happens automatically. If the times tables are shaky, division will be too, so check those first.

Fact families

3, 4 and 12 are a family: 3 × 4 = 12, 4 × 3 = 12, 12 ÷ 3 = 4, 12 ÷ 4 = 3. Make fact-family cards and say all four facts at once.

Practise with the games

  • Division in SumSum Playground always divides evenly, so children can build the basics without worrying about remainders.
  • Turn on just multiplication and division in Practice so the same families come up in both directions.
  • In True or False, check equations like "42 ÷ 6 = 8" by multiplying, which builds a checking habit too.

Be patient — Division needs an extra step of thinking backwards, so being slow at first is normal. Speed follows accuracy.
