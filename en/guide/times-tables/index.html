Learn times tables by understanding, not just memorising

Math practice tips · 5 min read · November 10, 2026

It looks like 81 facts to memorise, but once you understand a few ideas there are far fewer new ones to learn. Here is an order that is easier and sticks longer.

Multiplication is repeated addition

3 × 4 means adding 3 four times (3 + 3 + 3 + 3). Understanding this lets children rebuild a fact they have forgotten. Laying out four groups of three counters helps.

Swapping halves the work

3 × 7 and 7 × 3 have the same answer. That alone almost halves what you need to learn. Once you know the 3 times table, you already know 7 × 3.

Learn in order of difficulty

  1. 1s and 10s: the number itself, or add a zero.
  2. 2s: doubles.
  3. 5s: answers end in 0 or 5, which also links to reading a clock.
  4. 9s: the tens go up by one while the ones go down by one (09, 18, 27, 36…), and the digits always add up to 9.
  5. 4s: double the 2s.
  6. 3s and 6s: the 6s are double the 3s.
  7. 7s and 8s: swapping fills almost all of them in; the new ones are mainly 7 × 7, 7 × 8 and 8 × 8.

The tricky ones

6 × 7 = 42, 7 × 8 = 56, 6 × 8 = 48 and 8 × 9 = 72 trip up many children. "5, 6, 7, 8 → 56 = 7 × 8" is one handy memory hook.

Practise with the games

  • In Practice, turn on multiplication only and start with Easy (up to the 5s). Move on to Normal (up to 9s) and Hard (up to 12s).
  • Balloon Pop and True or False show you the answer to judge, which is great for checking what you've learned.
  • Missed facts come back more often. When one reappears, slow down and get it right.

Five minutes a day is enough — Recalling facts for five minutes every day sticks far better than memorising for 30 minutes once. This is called retrieval practice.
