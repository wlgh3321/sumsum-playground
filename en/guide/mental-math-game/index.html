Mental math game — a three-minute daily routine

Find a game · 4 min read · November 11, 2026

Mental math is more about repetition than tricks. You do the same kind of calculation often enough that the answer arrives without effort. A mental math game packs many problems into a short time, which is what that practice needs.

Two kinds of mental math game

ChoosingTime Challenge, Balloon Pop, True or False. You find the answer among options. Good for speed and for reading expressions quickly.
TypingFalling Problems, 60-Question Race. You enter the number yourself, so you must finish the calculation exactly. This shows your real level.

Only choosing leads to guessing by estimate. Only typing gets tiring. Mix the two.

Three-minute routines by level

It has been a while

  1. True or False · Add and Subtract · Easy (1 minute)
  2. Time Challenge · Add and Subtract · Normal (1 minute)
  3. Balloon Pop · Multiply · Easy (1 minute)

The basics are fine, you want speed

  1. Time Challenge · all four operations · Normal (1 minute)
  2. Beat the Clock · all four operations · Normal (as long as you last)
  3. 60-Question Race · Multiply and Divide · Normal

You are confident

  1. Falling Problems · all four operations · Hard · Fast
  2. Ranked mode, aiming for the Hall of Fame

How to tell you are improving

  • 90% accuracy: when the result screen shows over 90%, raise the level or the speed one step.
  • Time Challenge count: same settings, more correct than a week ago.
  • 60-Question time: a falling time means basic facts are becoming automatic.
  • Missed list: problems that stay on it are your current weak spots.

The common mistake

Going faster at the cost of accuracy makes you slower, because fixing a wrong answer takes longer than getting it right. Speed follows once accuracy is settled. See speed and accuracy.
